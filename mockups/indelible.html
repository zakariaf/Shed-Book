Shed Book · direction 1 of 3 · offline lambing notebook · iOS + Android · dark-first

A shed at 3am is not a database, it is a night that will be impossible to reconstruct in the morning, and the discipline for exactly that is four hundred years old. In a ship's deck log, a pilot's logbook and every GxP-regulated record on earth the rule is identical and absolute: never erase, never white out — strike one line through the error so it stays legible, write the correction beside it, initial and date it. That is not bureaucracy, it is a design pattern for a record kept by an exhausted human at 4am, and it is precisely what the spec's safety rule 4 demands. So Shed Book is a book of nights: one ruled page per night, one row per event, a madder-red margin rule down the left carrying the auto-captured time. Delete does not delete. Edits do not overwrite. Nothing needs a Save button, because the row commits at the first keystroke and you can see it, in ink, one line above the one you are writing — which removes the real 3am failure mode, which is not slowness but doubt. And because a lambing is a forty-minute window rather than a form-filling event, the row stays open: you press one corner slab once per lamb as each arrives, and the birth type is derived from the strokes and labelled as derived.

Signature move. There is no delete. The delete button draws a single madder line through the entry and prints STRUCK 03:41 in the margin, and the struck row stays on the page, in the ewe's history and in the CSV export forever. Swipe-to-delete is not merely banned here — the concept of erasure does not exist in the product. And nobody ever chooses "triplet": you press one enormous corner slab once per lamb, like a tally counter, and the strokes print in the lamb column.

Position on the nine axes
NavigationA single vertical stream — one long page you fill downward, and nothing else. There are no screens: a ewe card is the book filtered to 412, the medicine book is the book filtered to treatments, the season summary is the book's totals footer. One structure, one surface, filters. No rail, no tabs, no stack — the index is a printed line in the stream.
DensityMedium and rhythmic. Seven or eight 64pt ruled rows visible at once plus the live row. Density comes from the grid, not from cramming: each row is sparse, but rows repeat, so a page reads as a table without ever being a screen of small text.
Typographic voiceTwo voices, strictly separated and stated in the app itself — an editorial serif with true tabular lining figures for the record (what has been written), a humanist sans for every control (what you press). "The book is set in the book face; the buttons are set in the machine face." Even at a glance in the dark you can tell history from target.
GeometryHairline-ruled ledger, with the hairlines raised to 2pt because the ruling is the entire structure. Horizontal rules in dim ink, one vertical madder margin rule 68pt from the left edge, a double rule under a night's total. No cards, no containers, no radius in the record; the only filled shapes in the app are buttons.
Colour strategyInverted-paper ink — three ink densities on near-black plus exactly one hue, a dull madder red doing three jobs: the margin rule, the strike-through and the query mark. Red anywhere means the record has something to say about itself. There is no green, no blue and no status palette at all; alive/dead is a word, not a colour, because a colour-coded death reads wrong at 4am through a wet freezer bag.
Data representationTextual list with a numerals-first left column and literal tally strokes in the lamb column. Tags and times in tabular figures so they align into scannable columns down the page; unset fields print as visible gaps rather than hiding behind an empty form field; the season spread is small multiples of the same ruled row, not a dashboard.
Primary inputStepper-and-counter over fill-the-row. One 160×140pt corner slab pressed once per lamb — the largest target in any of the three directions, because it is the one pressed hundreds of times a season — and every other cell in the live row is its own 64pt target opening an inline chooser inside the row. The row you are filling is the form. A keypad sheet appears only for the tag cell.
Layout anchorBottom-pinned document with the counter slab in the dominant-hand corner (mirrorable). The page fills top-down like a real book, but the live row is welded above the bottom edge — the record reads upward into history and your thumb only ever works at the bottom.
Ornament levelPrint-like ink and paper. Rules, margins, daggers, strikes, a printed page header (NIGHT OF 27 JULY · PAGE 3) and a printed footer on every export stating this is a notebook and not a compliance record. No texture, no fake grain, no shadow — the ornament is the ruling.

Body floor is 20px in the record and 19px in controls, both above the spec's 18pt floor. The 14px stamp is the only thing below 18px and it is never the sole carrier of its meaning — remove every stamp and the app still works. Nothing is italic: italic serifs smear under halation at low luminance.

Auto-captured time is stamped AUTO; an edited one is stamped EDITED with a dagger and prints both times — the original is never overwritten. A struck row never moves, collapses or fades; it stays at 5.75:1 tonight, next year and in the CSV, and in red-shift the strike line doubles. A mis-pressed slab is not undone by a decrement: the third stroke is ruled through and the count re-prints as TWIN (COUNTED, 1 STRUCK), because a tally that can go down is not a tally.
